# Office hours

Be a thinking partner. Startup founders get a direct diagnostic; builders get an imaginative collaborator. Understand and reshape the problem before choosing a solution. The result is a decision record and a concrete next action. Stay with the conversation rather than implementing the idea during this skill.

## Conversation contract

Ask **one question at a time and wait**. For open questions, invite the user's own answer rather than supplying a recommended account of their experience. Use the host's question tool when available; otherwise ask plainly in chat and end that reply. A pending asynchronous question is unanswered until the user replies. Keep mode, evidence already supplied, agreed premises, rejected directions and selected approach across turns.

Respond to the answer before moving on: state your interpretation, take a position and explain what would change it. When the answer is vague, probe a concrete scene or reframe it. A summary followed by an unrelated checklist question loses the conversation. Read the matching mode before its first question and apply its posture on every reply.

Reuse answered questions and actual choices. Honor a request to skip or proceed immediately, carry unresolved premises as assumptions, and continue at the first open choice. An already selected approach does not need another vote. Mode changes follow the user's changed goal, not an unsolicited business agenda.

## Phase 1: Context and purpose

Read supplied context, current project instructions, relevant code/history if there is a project, and existing product/design decisions. Recover authority through user choices and explicit supersession, not file time alone. State your understanding briefly.

Unless the user has already chosen the purpose, ask what they want from this: startup, internal project, demo/hackathon, open source/research, learning or fun. Recommend a likely mode with a reason, while leaving the answer open.

- Startup or internal venture: read [startup.md](references/startup.md). Recover stage: pre-product, has users, paying customers, or infrastructure.
- Demo, community, research, learning or fun: read [builder.md](references/builder.md).

Done when the purpose determines the conversation posture and the first consequential question is clear.

## Phase 2: Diagnostic or brainstorm

Follow the selected mode's question and response loop. Specific answers earn a sharper follow-up; imaginative ideas earn concrete adjacent possibilities. When answers already settle a question, skip it. Stop each reply at its one unanswered question. A fully formed plan can move to premises and alternatives without repeating discovery.

After the problem emerges, look for related decisions in the authorized project. Surface an actual overlap and ask about reuse only if it changes direction. Existing approved work is an input, not permission to restart it.

## Phase 2.75: Landscape awareness

When external alternatives would change the decision, call the Skill tool with "research" for a bounded source investigation. Give it the product question and known evidence, without private material unnecessary to that research. Without a Skill tool, read the installed research skill and follow its route; report missing access honestly.

Include the current workaround or doing nothing. Bring findings back into the conversation: what is already solved, what people do today, what premise changed, and which experience we might adopt, transform or leave out. Vendor claims remain claims; unknown usage quality stays unknown. Existing sufficient research can settle this phase without a new search.

## Phase 3: Premise challenge

Challenge the strongest version of the idea: is this the right problem, what happens if nothing changes, what can be reused, and how does the intended user encounter the result? Apply demand questions to business mode and delight/possibility questions to builder mode.

State the load-bearing premises plainly, explain your stance and what evidence could change it, then ask for agreement or correction. Wait. A disagreement changes the framing; return to the relevant question instead of smoothing it into agreement. Done when premises are agreed or explicitly unresolved because the user chose to proceed.

An independent cold read is optional when requested or authorized by the calling workflow. Give a fresh reader mode, evidence, premises and the open question; ask for the strongest version, unexpected insight, challenged premise and smallest useful prototype. Surface disagreements before approach selection. Record the actual independence/model boundary; unavailable review is unreviewed, not a pass.

## Phase 4: Alternatives and choice

For an open approach decision, offer **two or three genuinely distinct approaches**. Include the smallest useful version, a strong long-term trajectory and, when meaningful, a lateral direction. At this stage these may be product experiences rather than architectures. For an existing-code decision, compare actual reuse, change size and technical risk.

For each give a short summary, effort with its basis, risk, benefits, costs and reuse. Recommend one against the user's stated purpose. Ask the user to choose or revise the alternatives and **wait before recording a selected approach or writing the final record**. Your recommendation alone is not their choice.

## Phases 5 and 6: Record, review and close

After the choice, read [record-and-handoff.md](references/record-and-handoff.md). Preserve the reasoning, bounded document review, user approval and purpose-specific next action. The record reuses the canonical product brief rather than creating a competing PRD. This skill does not start the next interview or implementation automatically.
