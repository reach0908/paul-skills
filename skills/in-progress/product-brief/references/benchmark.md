# From references to product choices

Read when competitor or reference evidence should inform this product. Clarify the decision and comparison lens from the request. Include the current workaround and relevant direct or indirect alternatives, with no fixed competitor quota.

For missing facts within the authorized task, call the Skill tool with "research" for the bounded question. Without that tool, read the installed research skill. Use its source route and evidence discipline rather than a second retrieval workflow. Existing cited research is sufficient when it answers the question.

For each consequential reference, connect:

| Reference and source/date | Verified fact, vendor claim or observed behavior | Experience principle | Adopt / transform / leave out proposal | Affected decision or next question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Explain what the user can do or feel differently, not just that another app has a feature. A visual metaphor may inspire a new experience, while its layout and interaction details stay with design. Keep unknown weaknesses unknown; lack of a vendor feature claim is not proof of bad usability. Do not invent market share, retention or defensibility to complete a matrix.

Distinguish matching an expected capability from a differentiating hypothesis. Recommend a choice with its trade-off and what evidence would change it. Record a user's selected implication as a decision, other implications as proposals. Update the canonical brief and its downstream impact only within the requested scope.
